How Jaini works

A plain-language explanation of the technology and data behind Jaini.

Step 1 — Scan or enter a barcode

You point your phone camera at a product barcode (UPC-A 12-digit or EAN-13 13-digit), or type it manually. The barcode number is extracted on your device — no image is ever uploaded to our servers.

Step 2 — Barcode lookup

The barcode number is sent to Jaini's API. The API searches a database of over 1.5 million products sourced from Open Food Facts, USDA FoodData Central, and other public food databases. If a match is found, the product name, brand, and ingredient text are retrieved.

Step 3 — Ingredient analysis

The ingredient text is analysed by a rules-based engine that checks for:

  • Red (Not Jain-friendly): Meat, poultry, fish, seafood, gelatin from animal sources, rennet, lard, and similar clearly non-Jain ingredients.
  • Yellow (Jain-restricted vegetables): Onion, garlic, leek, shallot, chive, scallion, potato, carrot, radish, turnip, beetroot, and other root or underground vegetables that many Jains avoid.
  • Orange (Needs verification): Egg, egg white, egg yolk, albumin, and ingredients with unclear animal origins such as "natural flavors," "mono and diglycerides," "L-cysteine," and similar.
  • Green (Likely Jain-friendly): No ingredients in the above categories were found in the available data.

Step 4 — Result and confidence

The result is returned with:

  • A status label (Likely Jain-friendly / Contains Jain-restricted vegetables / Needs verification / Not Jain-friendly / Not enough data)
  • A one-sentence explanation of what triggered the status
  • A confidence level based on how complete and clear the available ingredient data is
  • A breakdown of which ingredients fell into which category

What Jaini cannot do

  • Verify the accuracy of ingredient data provided by manufacturers or third-party databases
  • Detect cross-contamination or shared manufacturing equipment
  • Account for regional product formulation differences
  • Guarantee that a product hasn't changed its recipe since the database was last updated
  • Provide religious rulings — only informational guidance based on commonly listed Jain dietary restrictions

Data sources

  • Open Food Facts — A free, open, collaborative food database (openfoodfacts.org)
  • USDA FoodData Central — The US Department of Agriculture's food composition database
  • Additional sources added over time

How to help improve Jaini

If you find a product that is missing or incorrectly classified, use the "Report incorrect classification" or "Submit missing product" buttons on the result screen. You can also email hello@swapncore.com. Every report helps.
